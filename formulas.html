Formulas

Adjusted offensive efficiency Off Eff*

Game offense = points scored per 100 possessions − (opponent adjusted defense − national average)

Off Eff estimates scoring per 100 possessions against an average Division I defense. Higher is better. Each eligible game receives equal weight.

Adjusted defensive efficiency Def Eff*

Game defense = points allowed per 100 possessions − (opponent adjusted offense − national average)

Def Eff estimates points allowed per 100 possessions against an average Division I offense. Lower is better.

Both statistics use completed Division I vs. Division I games with valid points and possession data. Ratings are updated together using the previous iteration’s opponent ratings, with one national-average pseudo-game per team and 50% damping for stability. The calculation stops when the largest rating change is below 0.001; results stay blank if it does not converge. No venue adjustment is applied.

Adjusted efficiency margin = Off Eff − Def Eff

The national baseline uses total points divided by total possessions, multiplied by 100. The baseline anchor is separate from the preseason ranking blend. Raw efficiencies remain in the data export; the table displays adjusted efficiencies.

Net rating Net Rtg*

Net rating = adjusted offensive efficiency − adjusted defensive efficiency

The expected point margin per 100 possessions against an average opponent. Higher is better. For example, an Off Eff of 115 and Def Eff of 95 produce a net rating of +20.

Equivalently: (Off Eff − 100) + (100 − Def Eff). A defense above 100 reduces net rating; no absolute value is used. Net rating stays unavailable until both adjusted efficiencies are available.

Pace

Estimated possessions = FGA + 0.44 × FTA − OR + TO

FGA is field-goal attempts, FTA is free-throw attempts, OR is offensive rebounds, and TO is turnovers. This is a box-score estimate of possessions.

Game possessions = (team estimate + opponent estimate) ÷ 2
Pace = 40 × total game possessions ÷ total game minutes

Pace measures estimated possessions per 40 minutes, accounting for overtime. It uses completed games with both teams’ required box-score statistics and a valid game duration. Missing data is excluded, not replaced with zero. Pace is not adjusted for opponent strength.

Preseason weight

Preseason weight = max(0, (12 − games played) ÷ 12)

The preseason component fades by 1/12 per completed game and reaches zero after each team’s 12th game. For blending, preseason ranks are converted to a 0–100 score: 100 × (N − rank) ÷ (N − 1).

Overall score = preseason weight × preseason score + (1 − preseason weight) × in-season score

The current table is preseason-only. The in-season overall model has not yet been selected; PPT will not be substituted for it.

Points per turnover PPT

PPT = PPG ÷ turnovers per game

PPT divides average points per game by average turnovers per game. Both averages use the same games with available points and turnover data. Higher PPT ranks higher.

PPT measures scoring relative to turnovers. It does not adjust for opponent strength or pace. PPT also equals total points divided by total turnovers across those games. If average turnovers are zero, PPT is unavailable.

Games with missing points or turnover data are excluded.
